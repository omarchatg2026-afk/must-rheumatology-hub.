Abstract

Background. Awareness of rheumatic diseases and safe use of NSAIDs may affect care seeking and medication decisions.

Methods. A cross-sectional survey of a convenience sample aged 16–80 years on the MUST campus in Giza, Egypt, from April to July 2026. An anonymous, self-administered questionnaire assessed rheumatology knowledge and NSAID practices. Good knowledge was a score of at least 70%.

Results. Among 1,271 participants the mean score was 54.7% (SD 25.2); 385 (30.3%) reached good knowledge. Medical students and physicians scored higher than others (64.7% vs 48.5%). Medical participants reported more self-medication (21.0% vs 13.4%) and more use without a prescription (41.2% vs 34.6%).

Conclusion. Knowledge was limited, particularly outside the medical group. Medical training was associated with higher knowledge but not with safer NSAID practice. Education should cover the systemic manifestations of rheumatic disease and safe NSAID use. The design does not establish causality.
